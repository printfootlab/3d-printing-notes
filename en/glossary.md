# Glossary

## Processes

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| SLA (stereolithography) | Resin printing that uses a laser to trace and cure each layer |
| DLP (digital light processing) | Resin printing that uses a projector to cure a whole layer at once |
| LCD / MSLA | Resin printing that uses an LCD screen as a mask to expose a whole layer at once |
| CLIP / DLS | Continuous resin printing that uses an oxygen-permeable window, so the part is drawn upward without a separate peel step for each layer |
| FDM (fused deposition modelling) | Printing by extruding melted plastic filament through a nozzle |
| SLS / MJF | Powder-bed processes that fuse thin layers of polymer powder with a laser (SLS) or with a fusing agent and heat (MJF) |
| Build platform | The plate the part is printed onto; in bottom-up resin printers it rises out of the vat as the part grows |
| Supports | Temporary structures that hold up overhangs and anchor the part; removed after printing |

## Materials

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Elastomer | A polymer that can stretch or compress a lot and return to its original shape |
| Photoinitiator | The resin ingredient that absorbs light and starts the curing reaction |
| Green part | A resin part straight from the printer: shaped, but not yet fully cured |
| Post-cure | Curing a washed resin part with light and/or heat to reach its final strength |
| Dual-cure resin | A resin shaped by light during printing that reaches its final properties after a second, usually thermal, cure |
| Thermoplastic | A polymer that softens when heated and can be re-melted, such as TPU |
| Thermoset | A polymer that forms a permanent cross-linked network when cured and cannot be re-melted, such as cured resin |
| TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane) | A flexible thermoplastic used as filament and as powder for elastomer parts |
| Shore A / Shore D | Durometer hardness scales: Shore A for softer materials, Shore D for harder ones |
| Compression set | The share of a compression a material fails to recover after being held squeezed; lower is better for cushioning |

## Lattices and files

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Lattice | A structure made by repeating a cell of struts or walls through a volume |
| Unit cell | The smallest repeating piece of a lattice |
| Strut | A single thin beam in a lattice |
| Node | The point where struts meet; cracks often start here |
| Relative density | The share of a lattice's volume that is solid material |
| Bending-dominated lattice | A lattice whose struts mainly bend under load; softer than a stretch-dominated lattice of the same density |
| TPMS (triply periodic minimal surface) | A family of smooth surfaces that repeat in three directions, including the gyroid, Schwarz P and Schwarz D |
| Gyroid | A TPMS lattice with smooth walls and open channels, valued for even stress and easy drainage |
| Voronoi lattice | An irregular lattice whose cells are generated from scattered points, which makes gradual changes in cell size easy |
| STL | A widely used 3D file format that stores a surface as triangles only |
| 3MF | A more compact 3D printing file format that can also store units, materials and other information |
