# Post-processing resin lattice parts

**English** | [中文](../zh/04-树脂件后处理.md)

A resin part is not finished when the printer stops. It is covered in uncured liquid resin, still attached to supports and not yet at full strength. Lattices make each step harder, because there is so much surface and so many small spaces for resin to hide in.

> **Key points**
> - Six steps: drain, wash, dry, remove supports, post-cure, inspect.
> - Lattices trap liquid resin; every cell must be flushed, or it cures shut later.
> - Follow the resin maker's cleaner, washing time and cure settings.
> - Wear gloves and eye protection, ventilate, and never pour resin down the drain.

---

## 1. Drain

Before washing, let excess resin run off the part, ideally while it is still on the build platform or hung so the lattice channels point downward. Resin that drains back into the vat is not wasted, and less of it reaches the washing liquid.

## 2. Wash

Wash the part in the solvent or cleaner recommended by the resin maker. Isopropyl alcohol (IPA) is common for many resins; some, especially certain elastomers, need a specific cleaner. Typical methods are agitated baths, ultrasonic cleaners and spraying.

For lattices in particular:

- Liquid must flow through every cell. Agitation, turning the part and spraying through the channels all help.
- Washing too long can soften or swell some resins. Keep to the recommended time.
- Some production lines blow off or spin off resin with air before, or instead of, solvent washing.
- Resin left inside a lattice cures later, closing cells and creating stiff spots.

## 3. Dry

Let the solvent evaporate fully before curing. Solvent left in the part can cause cloudiness, cracking or a sticky surface.

## 4. Remove supports

Some resins release supports more cleanly before post-cure and others after; check the resin's guidance. Elastomer parts flex, so cutting supports close to the surface without tearing the part takes care.

## 5. Post-cure

Finish curing with light, heat or both, as the resin requires. See [02 Photopolymer resins](02-photopolymer-resins.md). For dual-cure elastomers, the oven step is what gives the final properties. Lattices cure fairly evenly because light and heat reach the thin struts easily, but very dense regions may need longer.

## 6. Inspect

| Check for | Common cause |
|---|---|
| Broken or missing struts | Usually near supports and edges |
| Filled cells | Trapped resin or overexposure |
| Tacky areas | Incomplete washing or curing |
| Dimensional error | Parts can shrink slightly during curing; compare with the design |

## Handling resin safely

Uncured resin can irritate skin and cause allergic sensitisation with repeated contact.

- Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection when handling liquid resin and wet parts.
- Work in a ventilated area, especially with solvents such as IPA, which are also flammable.
- Do not pour liquid resin or dirty solvent down the drain. Cure leftover resin with light and dispose of it according to local rules.
- Read the safety data sheet (SDS) for each resin and cleaner you use.
